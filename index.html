01 / MONITORING VALIDITY

SIEVE


Implemented audit engine

A good probe score is only the beginning.

SIEVE turns probe and intervention evidence into a scoped assessment of what a safety signal actually supports. The engine checks surface confounds, matched controls and measurement quality before allowing stronger causal claims.

What this contributes

A reproducible way to distinguish a decodable signal from evidence of causal sufficiency, while making missing evidence explicit.

Methods, relevance & limits

Why it matters

Frontier-model monitoring needs more than impressive benchmark scores. SIEVE makes the assumptions behind a proposed signal inspectable through evidence cards and controlled comparisons.

What it does not establish

It audits the evidence supplied, not every choice made during probe development. Causal sufficiency is distinct from predictive usefulness; a negative causal result does not by itself make a monitor useless.

02 / MONITORING VALIDITY

RAMP


Implemented audit toolkit

Will the signal survive a fairer test?

RAMP audits monitoring probes under leakage-free cross-fitting, independent labels and distribution shift. It packages uncertainty estimates and evaluation results into Signal Validity Cards, so a strong score is accompanied by the conditions under which it holds.

What I learned

Audits separated leakage-inflated performance from signals that held only within the evaluated distribution. The useful output is a bounded claim, not a single leaderboard number.

Methods, relevance & limits

Why it matters

A deployed monitor encounters new prompts and behaviors. Testing selection leakage and generalization helps determine whether measured performance is likely to transfer beyond the original evaluation.

Scope of the evidence

The audited cases do not establish robust production detection. Results are specific to the datasets, labeling procedures and shifts tested.

03 / MODEL BEHAVIOR

Alignment Evals


Completed studies · ongoing research

What changes when a model thinks it is being evaluated?

A series of studies spanning prompt framing, visible reasoning traces, activation probes and controlled steering. A newer 900-prompt paired audit examines harmful-request refusal alongside appropriate abstention, instruction following and general capability.

What I learned

Evaluation-related information was decodable across two open-weight models. In the full matched-control Qwen3 study, steering the recovered direction did not produce a distinct behavioral effect beyond the controls.

Methods, relevance & limits

Why it matters

Evaluation awareness can complicate how we interpret a model’s behavior. These studies test both the observable effects of evaluation cues and what internal signals can actually explain.

Scope of the evidence

Full matched-control steering was tested on Qwen3-32B at one layer. The newer hosted-model comparison found a large refusal reduction with an abstention cost, but provider differences prevent attributing the effect to a weight intervention alone.

04 / AGENT OVERSIGHT

Monitor Collusion


Exploratory development study

Can an investigator distinguish evidence from an adversarial account?

Built an environment where instructed adversarial agents can revise evidence around an executed workflow. Communicating and disconnected groups develop attacks over two rounds, while an investigator assesses the resulting record.

What I learned

Reviewing apparent attack successes exposed an underspecified evidence contract. The study did not establish confirmed investigator manipulation: the investigator’s warranted certainty was not adequately defined.

Methods, relevance & limits

Why it matters

As agents investigate other agents, detection alone is too narrow a success criterion. This work separates anomaly detection, authorization, attribution and false clearance, and examines what an investigator is entitled to infer.

Scope of the evidence

The targets were explicitly instructed to act adversarially. This is not evidence of spontaneous misalignment or a communication advantage. The latest work is a local development study; the public repository also contains earlier experiments.

05 / AGENT SECURITY

AgentWard


Tooling & external security report

Permissions should not depend on a model saying no.

AgentWard enforces boundaries around agent tools outside the language model, including least-privilege permissions, argument-level controls, approval flows and audit trails. I used it to review Google’s MCP Toolbox for Databases.

What I learned

I reported SQL-execution and permission risks. Maintainers acknowledged the report and later linked their released read-only controls back to the issue.

Methods, relevance & limits

Why it matters

Tool-using agents connect model behavior to real actions. Enforced permissions provide a separate layer of protection, while the Toolbox report demonstrates an application to an external project.

Scope of the evidence

The maintainers implemented the read-only feature. Their response does not confirm that every reported concern was resolved, or establish sole attribution for the change.
